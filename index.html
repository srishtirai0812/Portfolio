About

Problem first, then the product

I like starting from the problem: who is using this, what do they need to see, and what should they never be able to touch? That thinking shows up in my work, from role-based access in a multi-tenant supply chain system to the dashboards in an EdTech monitoring platform.

I also enjoy making data and AI usable. I trained a loan-risk classifier and explained its decisions with visuals, and I cleaned and validated a 7.7-million-row dataset. That is the kind of analytical work I want to do in analyst roles, where the job is to find the signal in messy data and explain what it means to the people who rely on it.

I’m equally drawn to technology consulting and implementation roles, where the work is to understand a client’s problem, turn it into a technical solution and explain it clearly to people who don’t live in code. Building an AI resume analyzer that is live on the web, and volunteering as a digital-literacy instructor, gave me practice at exactly that: turning technical ideas into something people can use.
